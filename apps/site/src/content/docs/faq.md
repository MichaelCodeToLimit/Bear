## Is Bear really free?

Yes. Bear is open source under the MIT license. There is no subscription, no account and no trial period.

## Is Bear affiliated with Microsoft?

No. Bear is an independent project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft. It reads and writes the common `.docx`, `.xlsx` and `.pptx` file formats so you can swap files with other people.

## Which systems does Bear run on?

Windows, macOS and Linux. See [Install](../get-started/install/) for the right download and the first-run steps for each.

## Why does my computer warn me when I open the installer?

Bear's installers are not signed with a paid certificate yet, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper show a warning the first time. The [Install](../get-started/install/) page walks through the click-throughs.

## Where are my files?

Documents, spreadsheets and presentations are normal files on your computer, saved wherever you choose. Notes, Tasks, Board and Sites keep their data in Bear's own data folder.

## Does Bear send my data anywhere?

Bear has no account and no cloud. The one exception is Mail, which talks to your own mail provider, and only when you have added an account.

## Can I open files from other office apps?

Yes. Write opens and saves `.docx`, Sheets opens and saves `.xlsx` and `.csv`, and Slides opens and saves `.pptx`. Complex formatting can look slightly different, so check important documents.

## Which apps are ready?

Write, Sheets, Slides, Notes, Mail, Sites, Board and Tasks. Chat, Insights, Drive and Forms are still planned.

## Can my team share a site?

Not yet. Sites work on your own computer today, and sharing needs a server that has not been built yet.

## I found a bug or want a feature

Please open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/MichaelCodeToLimit/Bear/issues). Pull requests are welcome too. Start with [Dev setup](../contribute/dev-setup/).
